What Happens at the Lab?

We live in a time when there's endless information about money and investing. You could say we're drowning in it.

So if the information is already at our fingertips, why do so many of us still hold back when it comes to doing something with our money, like investing it?

Maybe what we're missing is the confidence to act, even when the information is there.

The Money Research Lab started with one question. Why do smart, educated, independent women who know they need to do something with their money still hold back from taking the first step?

To find answers, the Lab goes out and meets as many women as it can. Through those conversations, it tries to understand what we think and feel, and what gets in the way between wanting to do something with our money and actually doing it.

If we can break that gap down, find the barriers, and give them names, we can start building tools and solutions around what women actually need.

Remember how Waze asked drivers to help map the roads? The Lab works on a similar idea. We're mapping how women think, feel, and make decisions about money and investing.

And that's where you come in.

The Lab needs your profile. Your story, your questions, what you're weighing up, what's holding you back, and what's worked for you. Maybe you've already started investing. Maybe you're still on the fence.

As more women share their stories, we can hear more points of view, spot patterns, and test ideas that could become tools to help women go from knowing they need to do something to actually doing it.

The more women who build wealth of their own and get to choose how they live, the better off the world will be.
Behind the Lab

I'm Moran

I'm Moran Shaked, a product designer (UX/UI), a mom of two, and a die-hard Excel fan.

I'm no economist, financier, or investment adviser. But for more than a decade, I've been trying to understand why women don't engage with our money the way we can, the way we should.

It started in 2013, when I went to Israel's first Bitcoin hackathon. I was the only woman in the room. I pitched an idea and my team took first place. One question stayed with me long after the prize.

Where were all the women?

Since then, I've founded Miss Bitcoin, wrote Financial Candy, run workshops, talked to women, done research, tried things, failed, and learned.

I believe money is so much more than a number in a bank account. It's the freedom to choose how we live, what we do, where we work, when we leave, and what we build for ourselves.

I call it “the business of opening up options.”

I want to open up the option first.
Then we'll see what we do with it.

I want women to build enough wealth of their own that they don't have to stay somewhere that isn't good for them, that they can go after their dreams, and above all, that they get to choose.

After more than a decade of questions, experiments, and projects, I started The Money Research Lab to keep looking for answers. This time, I want to do it systematically, with the women I'm building for.
